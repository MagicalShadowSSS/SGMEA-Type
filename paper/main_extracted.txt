 A B S T R A C T
Multimodal entity alignment (MEA) aims to identify equivalent entities across multimodal knowledge graphs. 
However, existing entailment-based approaches fail to capture entity-aware cross-modal semantics due to 
coarse representation and rigid fusion. In this paper, we propose an innovative Hierarchical Semantic-
Preserving (HSP) method, which holistically addresses semantic collapse, cross-modal misalignment, and rigid 
fusion by preserving high-dimensional visual structure, calibrating heterogeneous representations in a shared 
semantic space, and adapting fusion dynamically to entity types. Specifically, HSP first designs a hierarchical 
vision-preserving encoder to preserve the intrinsic topology of high-dimensional visual representations, 
ensuring structural integrity and fine-grained semantic retention in the preserve stage. Then, HSP constructs 
a cross-modal semantic calibration network to align textual and visual modalities within a unified semantic 
space in the calibrate stage, mitigating cross-modal discrepancies. Finally, in the adapt stage, HSP develops an 
entity-type-adaptive fusion controller to dynamically balance modality contributions through meta-learning, 
enabling flexible, type-aware multimodal integration. Extensive experiments conducted on three public cross-
lingual MEA benchmark datasets (ZH-EN, JA-EN, FR-EN) and two cross-graph datasets (FB-DB, FB-YG) under 
three experimental settings demonstrate that the proposed model achieves up to a 2% improvement in MRR 
over strong baseline methods.
1. Introduction
Knowledge graphs (KGs) provide a structured representation of 
semantic knowledge by mapping real-world entities to graph nodes, 
with edges explicitly modeling the relationships among them. Since 
the deployment of large-scale knowledge graphs [1], numerous studies 
have advanced their construction and application, particularly in tasks 
such as question answering [2], recommender systems [3], and natural 
language understanding [4], where KGs have become indispensable 
tools. However, with the rapid proliferation of multimedia content 
on social media platforms, traditional text-based knowledge graphs 
exhibit inherent limitations in leveraging and representing multimodal 
information. To address this issue, Chen et al. [5] propose Multimodal 
Knowledge Graphs (MMKGs) associating entities and relations with 
multimodal signals, enabling effective representation of real-world sce-
narios through cross-modal information aggregation MMKGs enable 
richer and more context-aware knowledge representations, thereby sup-
porting advanced applications such as visual question answering [6,7]

entity alignment task as an entailment reasoning problem, where the 
multimodal description of one entity semantically entails that of an-

pre-trained language model, this entailment-based formulation enables 
fine-grained, token-level interactions between entity representations. 
Consequently, it achieves more robust cross-modal semantic align-
ment and demonstrates significantly superior performance compared 
to contemporary methods [16–18].
However, the aforementioned entailment-based framework
inevitably encounters three critical deficiencies: (1) dimensionality 
catastrophe in cross-modal semantic mapping, (2) lack of cross-modal 
semantic consistency, and (3) limited adaptability in modality-specific 
fusion. Specifically, visual modalities encode rich fine-grained seman-
tics, such as spatial layout, facial details, or architectural cues, but exist-
ing entailment-based methods typically compress these high-
dimensional features into low-dimensional textual spaces through lin-
ear projection, resulting in substantial semantic loss. Meanwhile, visual 
representations, which are continuous, contrast sharply with the dis-
crete, symbolic form of textual descriptions, and without a calibrated 
semantic bridge, models often align entities based on spurious co-
occurrence rather than genuine semantic correspondence. Moreover, 
current fusion strategies apply uniform weighting across all entity 
types, ignoring that ‘‘person’’ entities rely heavily on facial details, 
‘‘building’’ entities benefit from global structural patterns, and ‘‘abstract 
concept’’ entities may lack meaningful visual signals, rendering the 
fusion process rigid and suboptimal.
To address these limitations and advance the entailment paradigm, 
we propose a novel Hierarchical Semantic Preserving (HSP) method 
that reconstructs the alignment process via a three-stage ‘‘Preserve–
Calibrate–Adapt’’ mechanism. Specifically, HSP comprises three key 
components. First, the Hierarchical Vision-Preserving Encoder (HVPE) 
preserves the intrinsic topology of 2048-dimensional visual features 
through a residual hierarchical Transformer with three parallel paths 
capturing local, global, and semantic information. Second, the Cross-
Modal Semantic Calibration Network (CSCN) projects visual and textual 
representations into a shared semantic subspace via bilinear mapping 
and contrastive learning with InfoNCE loss and hard negative min-
ing. Finally, the Entity-Type Adaptive Fusion Controller (EAFC) lever-
ages meta-learning to generate entity-type-aware fusion weights via 
a gated attention mechanism, enabling context-sensitive, type-aware 
fusion across modalities. These components form a unified, interde-
pendent pipeline, where high-fidelity features support cross-modal cal-
ibration, and calibrated representations are refined through type-aware 
fusion, forming a coherent end-to-end framework for multimodal entity 
alignment.
To comprehensively evaluate the proposed model and its hierar-
chical semantic preservation (HSP) strategy, we conduct systematic 
experiments on three cross-lingual datasets (ZH–EN, JA–EN, FR–EN) 
and two cross-graph datasets (FB15K–DB15K, FB15K–YG15K) under 
three seed settings (20%, 50%, 80%), where results show that HSP 
achieves up to 2.0% MRR improvements over the strongest baselines 
on cross-graph benchmarks under low-resource settings, while yielding 
consistent gains of 0.2%–0.6% on cross-lingual datasets, demonstrating 
robust generalization across diverse language pairs. The remainder of 
this paper is organized as follows: Section 2 reviews related work on 
multimodal knowledge graphs and entity alignment. Section 3 presents 
the proposed model, detailing the HVPE, CSCN, and EAFC modules. 
Section 4 describes the experimental setup and reports empirical re-
sults, including ablation studies. Section 5 discusses limitations and 
outlines directions for future work. Finally, Section 6 concludes this 
work.
In summary, the main contributions of this paper are as follows:
• We identify three key limitations in existing entailment-based 
approaches that hinder effective cross-modal alignment across 
modalities: dimensionality imbalance, semantic inconsistency, 
and inflexible modality fusion.
• We propose Hierarchical Semantic-Preserving (HSP), an
entailment-based multimodal entity alignment method that in-
troduces a three-stage ‘‘Preserve–Calibrate–Adapt’’ paradigm to 
jointly mitigate dimensionality-induced semantic collapse; en-
force cross-modal semantic consistency; and enable entity-type-
aware adaptive fusion.
• Extensive experiments on multiple public MEA benchmarks
demonstrate that HSP achieves superior and consistently robust 
performance across both cross-lingual and cross-graph alignment 
scenarios.
2. Related works
In this section, we systematically review the developmental tra-
jectory of multimodal knowledge graphs, and then elaborate on the 
specifics of the multimodal entity alignment task.
2.1. Multimodal knowledge graph
Multimodal knowledge graphs integrate heterogeneous modalities 
such as text, images, audio, and video, overcoming the semantic limi-
tations of traditional text-based knowledge graphs [19]. Such graphs 
enable fine-grained, multi-perspective semantic modeling of entities 
while enhancing semantic richness and cross-modal understanding. 
Recent studies have explored this paradigm from multiple perspectives. 
Zhang et al. [20] propose an optimal transport-based knowledge graph 
embedding model to address a key challenge in multimodal knowledge 
graph embedding: direct fusion of heterogeneous modalities can dis-
rupt their intrinsic spatial structures, hindering semantic consistency

2.2. Multimodal entity alignment
Multimodal Entity Alignment (MEA) extends the traditional en-
tity alignment task from knowledge graphs to multimodal knowledge 
graphs by integrating heterogeneous modalities, such as text and im-
ages, thereby enabling cross-graph multimodal entity matching. Early 
work [5] introduces a three-stage paradigm comprising embedding 
encoding, feature fusion, and feature matching, establishing a foun-
dational framework for subsequent research. Later studies [11,23] 
recognize the impact of cross-modal joint embeddings on alignment 
accuracy. EVA [24] leverages visual information as anchors to per-
form unsupervised entity alignment, enhancing matching performance. 
MCLEA [23] applies contrastive learning strategies to efficiently learn 
multimodal entity representations, further improving alignment qual-
ity. IBMEA [25] incorporates a variational information bottleneck to 
reduce interference from redundant information and misleading cues 
within the multimodal fusion process. More recent research [26,27] 
continues to explore advanced modality fusion strategies: the former 
dynamically computes modality weights and calibrates them according 
to uncertainty, whereas the latter addresses weak visual signals and 
modality imbalance through cross-modal mapping combined with joint 
optimization losses.
In summary, existing approaches have achieved substantial ad-
vances in modality encoding and cross-modal fusion, yet they re-
main insufficient for capturing fine-grained cross-modal interactions. 
The proposed method addresses inherent limitations of the entity-
centric framework, enabling simultaneous enhancement of fine-grained 
cross-modal semantic consistency and alignment accuracy.
3. Methodology
3.1. Task objective
The objective of the proposed HSP model is to address three key 
limitations in existing entailment-based multimodal entity alignment 
methods: (1) semantic distortion caused by excessive compression of 
high-dimensional visual features into a text-centric space, (2) insuf-
ficient cross-modal interaction leading to weak semantic alignment, 
and (3) static fusion that ignores entity-type-specific modality rele-
vance. By mitigating these issues, HSP better preserves feature fidelity, 
cross-modal consistency, and entity-type awareness, thereby enhancing 
visual-language co-modeling performance.
3.2. Overview
The Hierarchical Semantic-Preserving (HSP) model addresses key 
limitations in existing multimodal entity alignment approaches through 
a Preserve–Calibrate–Adapt architecture that organizes feature process-
ing into a progressive and interdependent pipeline. As illustrated in 
Fig.  2, HSP first preserves the hierarchical structure of visual features 
in their original high-dimensional space to mitigate information loss, 
thereby providing high-fidelity inputs for cross-modal alignment. It 
then aligns heterogeneous visual and textual modalities into a shared 
semantic subspace via contrastive calibration, establishing a consistent 
semantic basis. Finally, it adaptively fuses multimodal representations 
according to entity types to produce unified embeddings. These em-
beddings are subsequently fed into a pre-trained language model for 
entailment classification, which outputs a confidence score to guide the 
final alignment decision.

3.3. Hierarchical vision-preserving encoder
To mitigate the dimensional catastrophe and semantic collapse 
in the prior method [15], which naively compress high-dimensional 
visual features into low-dimensional spaces, we design the Hierarchi-
cal Vision-Preserving Encoder (HVPE). Unlike prior approaches that 
directly project visual features such as the ResNet-152 outputs into 
low-dimensional PLM embedding spaces, HVPE adopts a two-stage 
architecture: the first stage operates in the original high-dimensional 
feature space to preserve topological structure and semantic richness, 
while the second stage generates PLM-compatible learnable prefixes to 
enable effective downstream fusion and reasoning.

3.4. Cross-modal semantic calibration network
To mitigate the modality discrepancy between continuous visual 
representations and discrete textual semantics, we propose the Cross-
Modal Semantic Calibration Network (CSCN). Its core objective is to 
project the high-dimensional visual representations produced by HVPE 
and the PLM-encoded contextual text representations 𝑇𝑒 into a shared 
semantic subspace. Semantic correspondence is further strengthened 
through contrastive learning. Unlike token-level interactions within 
the PLM [15], CSCN explicitly models and corrects cross-modal bi-
ases through calibration and fusion modules, yielding a robust shared 
semantic representation. The process consists of three steps: textual 
context modeling, cross-modal semantic mapping and calibration, and 
cross-modal contrastive learning.

4. Experiments
4.1. Datasets
To comprehensively evaluate the performance of the proposed HSP 
model,1 we conduct experiments on five representative benchmark 
datasets encompassing both cross-graph and cross-lingual scenarios. 
As summarized in Table  1, the FB15K-DB15K dataset is constructed 
by aligning entities between FB15K [28] and DB15K [29], result-
ing in a cross-kg entity alignment benchmark with 12,849 align-
ment seeds and 26,281 associated images. Similarly, the FB15K-YG15K 
dataset is constructed by matching identical entities between FB15K 
and YG15K [30], yielding a dataset with 11,199 alignment seeds and 
24,638 associated images. The DBP dataset [29] comprises three cross-
lingual sub-datasets: ZH-EN, JA-EN, and FR-EN, covering Chinese, 
English, French, and Japanese, and representing complex cross-lingual 
interaction scenarios; these sub-datasets contain 38,960, 26,480, and 
28,032 associated images, respectively, providing rich cross-modal 
interaction cues for entity alignment. For training, we adopt three 
different alignment seed ratios: 20%, 50%, and 80%, and for entities 
without associated images, we follow the prior work [24] setting by 
assigning random vectors as their visual features.
4.2. Implementation details
All experiments are implemented using the PyTorch framework2 
and conducted on a system equipped with two NVIDIA RTX 4090 GPUs, 
each with 24 GB of memory. To ensure reproducibility, we repeat each 
experiment using five distinct random seeds and report the average 
performance across three independent runs for each seed. The model 
is trained with a batch size of 32 to balance training stability and 
GPU memory consumption, for up to 100 epochs, with early stopping 
applied based on validation set performance to mitigate overfitting. We 
employ the Adam optimizer, using an initial learning rate of 3×10 −5 for 
the language encoder and 1 × 10−4 for other components; this learning 
rate is decayed by a factor of 0.1 every 20 epochs. A dropout rate of 
0.1 and weight decay of 1 × 10−5 are applied throughout the model to 
enhance generalization, and the GeLU activation function is used in all 
applicable layers.
To ensure a fair comparison with the state-of-the-art model [15], we 
reproduce its results using the original protocol. The original work used 
NVIDIA A6000 GPUs with 48 GB memory,3 while our experiments use 
RTX 4090 GPUs. Differences in hardware may cause minor performance 
variations. We retain the original results for reference and analyze 
discrepancies to support a transparent evaluation of HSP.

4.3. Evaluation metrics
To ensure a comprehensive evaluation of the proposed method on 
the multimodal entity alignment task, we adopt Hits@n and MRR as 
primary evaluation metrics. Hits@n measures the proportion of test 
entities for which the correct alignment appears within the top n 
ranked candidates; for instance, Hits@1 = 0.8 indicates that the correct 
match is ranked first in 80% of the cases. MRR, or Mean Reciprocal 
Rank, computes the average of the reciprocal ranks of correct entities, 
assigning scores of 1, 0.5, 0.33, and so on for ranks 1, 2, 3, respectively. 
This metric rewards models that place correct matches higher in the 
ranked list. Higher values of both Hits@n and MRR indicate more 
accurate and robust entity alignment performance.
4.4. Baseline models
To evaluate the effectiveness of the proposed model, we assess it 
against several recent and representative baselines on two cross-graph 
datasets. All hyperparameters follow the configurations reported in 
the original papers to ensure reproducibility. These baselines cover 
diverse paradigms, including multimodal fusion, contrastive learning, 
distribution alignment, and causal modeling: PoE [31] establishes a 
foundational framework for cross-modal semantic fusion; MMEA [5] 
unifies structure, attributes, and visual information for multimodal 
entity alignment; EVA [24] employs vision as a pivot for unsuper-
vised alignment; MCLEA [23] advances multimodal contrastive learn-
ing to align heterogeneous embedding spaces; MEAformer [32] formu-
lates a meta-modality hybrid mechanism for heterogeneous modality 
fusion; PCMEA [18] introduces pseudo-label calibration to alleviate 
noise propagation; LoginMEA [33] presents a local-to-global inter-
action network for multi-level relational fusion; OTMEA [16] aligns 
multimodal distributions via optimal transport to reduce heterogene-
ity; GSIEA [34] separately models graph structure and multimodal 
information to mitigate structural heterogeneity; RICEA [26] enhances 
generalization through relative interaction modeling and dynamic cal-
ibration; CDMEA [17] addresses modality bias from a causal perspec-
tive; ME3A [15] formulates MEA as an entity entailment task to enable 
fine-grained cross-graph interaction. On the DBP dataset, we evalu-
ate the model against MSNEA [35], MultiJAF [36], DESAlign [37], 
PSNEA [38], AMFSEA [13], and IBMEA [25].
4.5. Main results
We conduct extensive experiments across multiple datasets to eval-
uate the performance of the proposed HSP model against recent and 
competitive baselines. As shown in Table  2, HSP demonstrates highly 
competitive performance on the cross-graph datasets FB15K-DB15K and 
FB15K-YG15K under alignment seed ratios of 20%, 50%, and 80%, fre-
quently surpassing previous methods. In the low-resource setting with 
only 20% seed alignments, although some methods achieve slightly 
higher Hits@1 on FB15K-DB15K, HSP-M attains the best Hits@10 
(0.837) and MRR (0.702), indicating stronger overall ranking capabil-
ity and more reliable candidate alignment under limited supervision. 
As supervision increases, HSP’s advantage becomes more evident; for 
instance, with 50% seeds on FB15K-DB15K, HSP improves Hits@1 
to 0.755, outperforming the strongest baseline (0.735) by 2.0%, and 
maintains top performance across nearly all metrics at the 80% set-
ting. These results suggest that HSP scales effectively with additional 
supervision while preserving stable performance gains.
According to the results in Table  3, HSP consistently achieves 
leading performance across all language pairs (ZH–EN, JA–EN, FR–
EN). In particular, HSP-M attains an MRR of 0.946 and Hits@1 of 
0.906 on FR–EN, while consistently outperforming recent competitive 
baselines, including the ME3A variants, on the other subsets. These 
uniform improvements across linguistically diverse datasets indicate 
that HSP enhances both the discriminability of entity representations 
and the accuracy of alignment, which can be attributed to its coordi-
nated integration of structural preservation, semantic calibration, and 
adaptive feature fusion.
4.6. Ablation study
To assess the contribution of each component within the proposed 
HSP framework, we conduct systematic ablation studies on the DBP15K 
benchmark datasets (ZH–EN and JA–EN). As shown in Table  4, re-
moving any core module leads to performance declines of varying 
magnitudes. Specifically, eliminating HVPE (Hierarchical Visual Preser-
vation Encoder) results in the largest drop, with Hits@1 decreasing by 
0.038 on ZH–EN, suggesting that preserving high-dimensional visual 
topology is associated with reduced semantic representation degrada-
tion. Removing CSCN, which performs cross-modal calibration through 
bilinear interaction and contrastive learning, yields a performance 
decrease of 0.032, indicating that explicit semantic alignment within 
a shared subspace contributes positively to model performance. Dis-
abling EAFC (Entity-type Adaptive Fusion Controller) lowers Hits@1 by 
0.023, suggesting that dynamically adjusting modality weights based 
on entity semantics tends to be more effective than static fusion. 
In addition, removing meta-learning (w/o META) or contrastive cal-
ibration (w/o CONT) causes performance drops of 0.011 and 0.028, 
respectively, indicating their roles in supporting cross-entity-type gen-
eralization and cross-modal consistency. Overall, these observations are 
consistent with the ‘‘preserve–calibrate–adapt’’ design principle: HVPE 
is associated with maintaining structural information, CSCN supports 
semantic alignment across modalities, and EAFC enables context-aware 
fusion. Collectively, these components contribute to stable performance 
under low-resource and cross-lingual settings.
As shown in Table  5, we systematically evaluate the impact of differ-
ent pretrained visual and textual encoders on model performance and 
inference efficiency. For visual encoders, performance does not increase 
monotonically with parameter scale: VGG-16 and ViT-base contain 
138M and 87M parameters, respectively, both exceeding the 60M 
parameters of ResNet-152, yet they exhibit slightly inferior alignment 
performance. Although CLIP yields an approximate 0.6% improvement 
in MRR, it also introduces higher inference overhead. This observation 
may be related to the relative sparsity of visual signals in multimodal 
entity alignment, where most entities are associated with only a single 
image, thereby limiting the effective utilization of large-scale visual 
models. For textual encoders, replacing the static word embeddings 
of GloVe with contextualized pretrained language models leads to a 
substantial performance gain, improving MRR by approximately 5.9%, 
suggesting that dynamic semantic modeling helps capture entity con-
textual information. In contrast, BERT-large provides only a modest 
improvement of about 0.4% over BERT-base, indicating that further 
scaling the model yields limited returns under the current data con-
ditions. Overall, smaller encoders may be insufficient for modeling 
cross-modal semantics, whereas excessively large models are more 
likely to be constrained by data availability while incurring additional 
computational cost. Therefore, ResNet-152 and BERT-base offer a more 
balanced trade-off between performance and efficiency and are adopted 
as the default configuration.

4.9. Reproducibility analysis
In the main experiments, the proposed HSP consistently outper-
forms a range of recent competitive methods. However, we observe that 
several models exhibit performance fluctuations across different runs, 
indicating sensitivity to random initialization and training stochasticity, 
as shown in Fig.  5. To systematically assess model stability, we select 
multiple representative approaches and conduct repeated independent 
experiments on two cross-graph and two cross-lingual datasets. To 
reduce the influence of supervision strength on performance variance, 
all experiments are repeated five times under a moderate supervision 
setting.
As illustrated in Fig.  6, HSP demonstrates strong stability across 
multiple runs, with the variance of all evaluation metrics remaining 
below 1.5% and performance distributions highly concentrated. In 
contrast, although LoginMEA achieves relatively high peak perfor-
mance, it exhibits more noticeable fluctuations, with a variance of 
approximately 1.8%. Furthermore, DESAlign, RICEA, and ME3A show 
variances exceeding 1.4% across datasets, suggesting greater sensitiv-
ity to randomness. Overall, while maintaining favorable performance, 
HSP exhibits improved stability and consistency, resulting in smaller 
performance variations and more reliable generalization across both 
cross-lingual and cross-graph scenarios.

5. Limitations analysis
Although HSP achieves superior performance, it has three practical 
limitations. First, the HSP model relies on frozen ResNet-152 features 
as visual input. Removing HVPE reduces Hits@1 by 3.8% on DBPZH–EN, 
showing dependence on high-quality visual features; however, ResNet-
152 may provide insufficient representations for abstract or visually 
ambiguous entities (e.g., ‘‘inflation’’), limiting alignment. This limita-
tion could be mitigated by adopting stronger vision foundation models. 
Second, the EAFC component assumes a predefined set of entity types 
for adaptive fusion. Although meta-learning aids generalization, align-
ment of unseen or fine-grained types depends on an auxiliary type 
predictor, which may propagate errors into fusion weights, restrict-
ing applicability in open-world settings. Future work could explore 
type-free fusion mechanisms that adapt without explicit type super-
vision. Third, the model-agnostic meta-learning in EAFC introduces 
non-negligible training overhead due to bi-level optimization, which 
may limit scalability on ultra-large knowledge graphs under memory-
constrained batch processing. Future work could investigate first-order 
approximations or distributed strategies to alleviate this cost.
6. Conclusion
In this paper, we re-examine entailment-based multimodal entity 
alignment and identify three fundamental limitations that hinder its 
effectiveness: (1) semantic collapse caused by aggressive compres-
sion of high-dimensional visual features, (2) insufficient cross-modal 
semantic consistency due to weak calibration mechanisms, and (3) 
rigid fusion strategies that ignore entity-type-specific modality rele-
vance. To address these challenges, we propose HSP, a Hierarchical 
Semantic-Preserving framework that unifies the alignment process un-
der a Preserve–Calibrate–Adapt paradigm. Concretely, HSP preserves 
the intrinsic topology and fine-grained semantics of visual representa-
tions via a hierarchical vision-preserving encoder, thereby mitigating 
information loss during feature compression. It then enforces cross-
modal semantic consistency by explicitly calibrating visual and textual 
representations within a shared semantic space. Furthermore, HSP in-
troduces an entity-type-adaptive fusion controller that dynamically ad-
justs modality contributions; the fusion strategy is optimized via meta-
learning to capture context-dependent modality relevance. By jointly 
addressing semantic fidelity, cross-modal consistency, and adaptive 
fusion, HSP improves the reliability and expressiveness of entailment-
based alignment. Extensive experiments on five public benchmarks 
across cross-lingual and cross-graph scenarios show that it consistently 
outperforms competitive baselines under varying supervision levels, 
demonstrating strong effectiveness and generalization.
